Today's goal is to transition from global scrambling to Block-Wise Chaotic Scrambling, the core foundation of learnable image encryption.

Day 4 (12/9/26)
we will apply Information Entropy and Histogram Analysis to validate your cipher's strength.
Information entropy measures the degree of randomness in a system. For an 8-bit grayscale image (where pixel intensities range from 0 to 255), the ideal theoretical entropy is exactly 8.0. The closer our ciphertext gets to 8.0, the more resistant it is to statistical attacks.
$$H(m) = -\sum_{i=0}^{255} P(m_i) \log_2 P(m_i)$$

Result of day: Plaintext Entropy: 7.2976
Ciphertext Entropy: 7.6426  for B=8

Day 5(12/9/26)

complete the statistical validation of our learnable cipher. While Information Entropy proved the pixel distribution is random, we must now mathematically prove that the structural geometry is destroyed and that the cipher can survive differential attacks.

In meaningful plain images, adjacent pixels typically have a correlation coefficient very close to 1. An effective encryption system must disrupt this resemblance, bringing the correlation coefficient between adjacent pixels—whether horizontally, vertically, or diagonally—close to 0. This ensures that structural metrics like the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) also drop to zero, proving no visual geometry leaks through the cipher.

Differential Attack Analysis (NPCR & UACI)
A differential attack involves an adversary making a tiny, one-bit change to your original image to see how the resulting encrypted image changes. To resist these attacks, your encryption algorithm must be highly sensitive to the plain image. We measure this sensitivity using two standard cryptographic metrics
